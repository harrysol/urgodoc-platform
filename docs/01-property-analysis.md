| Data point | Where to get it |

| Zoning district + overlays | [City of Miami Beach Planning](https://www.miamibeachfl.gov/city-hall/planning/) |
| FEMA flood zone + Base Flood Elevation | FEMA Map Service Center (search the address) |
| Existing permit history on the house | Miami Beach / Miami-Dade building permit portal |

**Why it matters:** lot size + zoning district set your *maximum* allowable
height, setbacks, and floor-area ratio (FAR). Flood zone + Base Flood Elevation
can force the ground floor to be raised — which changes whether a second story
even fits under the height cap.

## Street View / site visual checklist (do this yourself)

When you look at Street View and your own photos, note:

- **Roof type** — flat, low-slope, or pitched/gable? A flat or low-slope 1955
  roof is often easier to build *up* from than a complex pitched roof.
- **Wall construction** — Miami Beach 1955 homes are usually **CBS** (concrete
  block + stucco). Confirm: block or wood frame? This drives the structural plan.
- **One continuous mass vs. additions** — has the house already been added onto?
  Older additions may not be able to carry load.
- **Adjacent setbacks** — how close are neighbors? Affects privacy/shadow rules
  and what the city will approve aesthetically.
- **Driveway / parking** — Miami Beach requires on-site parking; a bigger house
  may trigger an added parking requirement.
- **Grade / elevation** — does water pool near the house? Flood-zone clue.

## Output of this step

A one-page "site facts" sheet: folio #, lot sq ft, zoning code, max height,
flood zone + BFE, wall type, roof type. Everything downstream depends on it.
