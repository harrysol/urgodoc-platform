# 02 — Zoning, Permits & Code (Miami Beach / Miami-Dade)

> The house is in the **City of Miami Beach**, which has its **own Land
> Development Regulations** (not the City of Miami "Miami 21" code, and not the
> unincorporated Miami-Dade code). Building/structural enforcement is still under
> **Florida Building Code + Miami-Dade HVHZ** rules. Confirm specifics with the
> Miami Beach Planning Dept.

## The big constraints for going up

1. **Maximum height** — Miami Beach single-family districts cap building height
   (commonly measured from the required flood/grade elevation to the top of the
   roof). A second story plus a flood-raised ground floor can bump into this cap.
   *This is the #1 thing to confirm for your lot.*
2. **Setbacks** — front/side/rear minimums. A second floor sometimes has a
   **larger required setback** than the first (so it can't sit directly above the
   existing exterior walls — it may need to step back).
3. **Floor Area Ratio (FAR) / lot coverage** — total allowed conditioned floor
   area relative to lot size. Determines how big the second floor can be.
4. **Unity of title / parking** — added bedrooms can trigger an on-site parking
   requirement.

## HVHZ — the non-negotiable structural layer

Miami-Dade is a **High-Velocity Hurricane Zone**, the strictest wind code in the
US. For a vertical addition this means:

- All new windows/doors must be **impact-resistant (NOA-approved)**.
- Roofing products need a **Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA)**.
- The whole load path (new floor → existing walls → foundation) must be
  engineered for HVHZ wind uplift. **This requires a licensed engineer.**

## Flood / FEMA — CONFIRMED for this lot

The survey confirms **Flood Zone AE, Base Flood Elevation 8.0 ft (NGVD)** (FIRM
panel 12086C0307L). This is firm, not a maybe:

- **Finished floor must be at/above BFE 8.0′ + Miami Beach freeboard.** New/
  substantially improved construction must comply.
- A second-floor addition can be a **"substantial improvement"** (cost ≥ 50% of
  the structure's value) — which can trigger bringing the **whole house** into
  flood compliance (i.e., raising the ground floor). That interacts directly with
  the height cap: raised floor + two stories must still fit under max height.
- Mandatory **flood insurance**; elevation choices drive premiums.

## Waterfront (canal) considerations — CONFIRMED

The rear lot line is on a **canal**. That adds:

- **Waterfront setback** from the canal/seawall (Miami Beach has a specific
  rear/waterfront yard requirement — confirm the dimension with Planning).
- **Seawall** condition/ownership and any required repair/cap elevation.
- Building/zoning review may be more involved for waterfront parcels.
- The **south-line paver/fence encroachment** noted on the survey should be
  cleared up (survey/legal) before you finalize the footprint.

## Permit path (typical sequence)

1. **Survey + zoning verification** (you / surveyor).
2. **Architect schematic design** → check it fits zoning envelope.
3. **Structural engineering** for the addition (HVHZ load path).
4. **Signed & sealed permit set** (architect + engineer stamps). *Mandatory.*
5. **Submit to City of Miami Beach Building Dept.** for plan review.
   - May also need **Design Review Board / Planning** approval depending on scope.
6. **Permit issued → construction → inspections → CO (Certificate of Occupancy).**

## What this means for "saving millions" with AI

AI does **none** of steps 3–5. Those are legally gated behind FL licenses.
AI is hugely useful for steps before #2 — exploring the design and producing
renderings so you walk into the architect knowing exactly what you want. That's
where you cut wasted design fees and revisions, not where you skip the engineer.
