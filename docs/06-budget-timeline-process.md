# 06 — Budget, Timeline & Process

> Ballpark only — Miami Beach HVHZ work runs high, and a 1955 structure can hide
> costs. Treat these as ranges to plan around, then get real bids. Verify
> everything locally; do not commit money off these numbers.

## Rough cost ranges (second-floor addition, Miami Beach)

| Item | Rough range |
|------|-------------|
| Survey + structural site investigation | $1.5k – $5k |
| Architect (design + permit set) | ~8–15% of construction cost |
| Structural engineering (HVHZ) | included in/alongside architect fee |
| Construction — partial second story | varies widely by size/finish |
| Construction — full second story (pop-top, HVHZ) | typically **$300–$600+/sq ft** in this market |
| Permits + impact fees | low thousands+ |
| Contingency | **15–20%** (older home = higher) |

A full second story roughly the size of the current footprint is a **major
project** — budget conservatively and keep a real contingency. The structural
investigation up front is the cheapest way to avoid a six-figure surprise.

## Realistic timeline

| Phase | Rough duration |
|-------|----------------|
| Property/zoning research + AI concepts (DIY) | 1–3 weeks |
| Architect selection + fee proposals | 2–4 weeks |
| Schematic design → design development | 1–3 months |
| Structural eng. + signed/sealed permit set | 1–2 months |
| Miami Beach plan review / permitting | **2–6+ months** (plan for delays) |
| Construction | 6–12+ months |

## The order that keeps it from stalling

1. **Pull records + survey + flood zone** ([docs/01](docs/01-property-analysis.md)).
2. **Structural site investigation** — can it go up, and how? ([docs/03](docs/03-feasibility-second-floor.md))
3. **Confirm zoning envelope** — height, setbacks, FAR ([docs/02](docs/02-zoning-permits-miami.md)).
4. **DIY AI concepts + massing** ([docs/05](docs/05-ai-3d-rendering-guide.md)).
5. **Interview 3 architects, get fee proposals** ([docs/04](docs/04-architect-shortlist.md)).
6. **Hire architect → schematic → DD → signed/sealed permit set.**
7. **Permit submittal → review → issuance.**
8. **Bid construction (if not design-build) → build → inspections → CO.**

## Where the "save millions" actually comes from

- **Front-load the structural investigation** so you design the *right* (cheaper)
  approach — partial vs. full, reinforce vs. pop-top.
- **Do the concept/rendering phase yourself with AI** so architect fees go to
  engineering and stamping, not exploration.
- **Get 3 competitive fee proposals** and (if not design-build) competitive
  construction bids.
- **Right-size the addition** — a partial second floor can deliver most of the
  value at a fraction of a full pop-top's structural cost.

What you can't skip: the licensed architect, the structural engineer, the HVHZ
compliance, and the permit. Skipping those isn't a saving — it's an unpermitted,
uninsurable, unsellable house.
